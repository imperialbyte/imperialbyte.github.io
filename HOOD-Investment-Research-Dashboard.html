Accumulate on Current Levels — Hold for the Super App Decade
Rating: Strong Buy / Accumulate · Target: $119 (base) – $166 (bull) · Horizon: 3+ Years · April 2, 2026 @ $68.90
The Core Thesis

Robinhood has quietly engineered one of the most consequential pivots in consumer finance history. The company that went public in 2021 as a meme-stock gateway has rebuilt itself into a structurally profitable, structurally growing financial super app — and the market, still anchored to 2021 narratives, is pricing it as neither.

At $68.90, the stock sits 55% below its October 2025 high despite delivering two consecutive years of 50%+ revenue growth, achieving a 56.4% adjusted EBITDA margin, and generating $1.62B in free cash flow. The decline is entirely explained by crypto weakness and macro de-risking. The business has not deteriorated. The thesis has only strengthened.

Our rating is Accumulate on current weakness, Hold for the next several years as Robinhood's financial super app comes into full focus. This is not a trade — it is a multi-year bet on the company most likely to become the primary financial relationship for the next generation of American and global investors.

The ARPU Engine

Robinhood's monetization story is misunderstood. Most coverage focuses on funded customer count (+7.1% YoY in Q4 2025) and misses the real growth driver: ARPU expansion. ARPU grew +16.5% YoY to $191 in Q4 2025, while assets under custody surged +67% to $322B. Net deposits hit a record $68B in FY2025. The average customer is entrusting dramatically more of their financial life to Robinhood every year.

The Gold subscription ecosystem is the monetization flywheel. Gold subscribers grew +58.3% to 4.18M. Each Gold customer generates materially higher ARPU through margin lending, higher cash sweep rates, retirement matches, credit card rewards, and soon banking NII. As Gold penetration (currently 15.5% of funded customers) grows, the incremental economics compound without requiring new customer acquisition.

The New Product Velocity

What makes Robinhood exceptional is not any single product — it is the pace and quality of product launches:

Banking: $0 → $1.5B deposits in 4 months (Nov 2025 → Mar 2026). Average balance of $15K per customer — primary account behavior. Over 50% of early Banking users enrolled in direct deposit.

Strategies: $0 → $1.3B AUM in 7 months. Automated portfolio management with low friction. Complements TradePMR's $42.5B RIA AUC.

Prediction Markets: $300M+ annualized run rate in first year. 12B+ event contracts traded in 2025. Now owning the infrastructure via MIAXdx (CFTC-licensed exchange + clearinghouse) with Susquehanna as liquidity partner.

Gold Card: 600K holders, 5x growth in 2025. $10B in annualized spend. Credit card interest alone up 200% YoY in Q4 2025. Platinum Card launched March 2026 at $695/year — going directly after Amex Platinum.

Robinhood Ventures (RVI): NYSE-listed closed-end fund launched March 2026. Portfolio: Stripe, Databricks, ElevenLabs, Airwallex, Revolut, Ramp. First retail vehicle for pre-IPO access with no accreditation requirement.

Shiv Verma at Q4 earnings: "We are now up to 11 businesses with over $100M in annualized revenue." Several more approaching that threshold.

Regulatory Tailwinds

GENIUS Act (signed July 18, 2025): The US's first federal digital asset law. Establishes a framework for payment stablecoins. Robinhood already announced a stablecoin partnership with Paxos (US.DG). Legal certainty to build and distribute stablecoin infrastructure globally through Bitstamp.

CLARITY Act (passed House 294-134, advancing in Senate): Would grant CFTC exclusive jurisdiction over digital commodity spot markets. Robinhood's MIAXdx is CFTC-designated — making it a structural beneficiary of the new regime. Crypto trading under CFTC rules is historically far more business-friendly than SEC.

PFOF safe: New SEC Chair Paul Atkins withdrew the Gensler-era order competition rule and best execution proposals in June 2025. The existential PFOF risk that haunted HOOD from 2021-2023 has been eliminated.

Trump Accounts: US government considering Robinhood as trustee for government-funded $1K investment accounts for every newborn 2025-2028. Fidelity and Vanguard reportedly not on the initial candidate list. If selected, this creates a permanent, government-supported customer onboarding pipeline for a generation.

The Management Conviction Signal

On March 24, 2026, Robinhood's board authorized a $1.5 billion share buyback — adding $1.1B in new capacity to existing programs. This represents ~10% of current market cap. New CFO Shiv Verma called it an "opportunity to secure long-term value at a price that doesn't reflect the company's genuine potential." Simultaneously, JPMorgan revolving credit was expanded from $2.65B to $3.25B.

Management does not spend $1.5B buying back shares at $69 because they expect the business to deteriorate. This is the clearest possible signal of intrinsic value confidence from people with full information.

Why Robinhood Wins the Super App Race

Multiple companies have tried to build financial super apps. Most have failed because they started with banking and tried to add investing. Robinhood has the rarest possible starting position: 27 million active investors with real assets, real engagement, and real trust — and is adding banking, credit, advisory, private markets, and crypto infrastructure on top of that.

The switching cost grows with each product added. A user with their IRA, brokerage, Gold Card, banking, and credit card at Robinhood does not churn. The Gold subscription creates a psychological lock-in that traditional banks — who charge fees and give nothing back — cannot replicate. Every new product raises the value proposition and lowers the probability of attrition.

AI is Robinhood's silent margin expander: 75%+ of customer support cases resolved by AI, including complex licensed brokerage cases. Nine-figure savings in 2025. Cortex AI assistant rolling out in 2026 as both a consumer product and a trading co-pilot for Legend users.

In 2-3 years, we believe the market will recognize that Robinhood's TAM is not "retail brokerage" — it is the entire financial services relationship of the next generation. At that point, the valuation will reflect something much closer to a fintech infrastructure business than a trading app. The re-rating, when it comes, will be substantial.

Conclusion & Positioning

Accumulate on current weakness. Add on further dips. Hold through cycles. The thesis does not require a specific near-term catalyst — it requires patience as 11+ product lines compound and the market's perception of Robinhood converges with the business reality.

The bear case has a 38%+ floor from FCF growth alone (20x FY2028E FCF at $95). The base case returns +72%. The bull case returns +141%. The super bull scenario — Trump Account trustee selection, Clarity Act passage, Social flywheel, private markets scale — could deliver 200%+.

Thesis invalidators: PFOF ban without offset, core customer attrition at scale, prolonged 3+ year crypto/equity bear, or management credibility loss. None of these are base case.

Post-Earnings Reactions
The Son of Immigrants Who Rewired Wall Street
Vlad Tenev, Baiju Bhatt, and the mission to democratize finance for all
1987 — Sofia, Bulgaria
Vladimir Tenev Is Born
Vladimir Tenev is born in Sofia, Bulgaria, to a family that would soon make the journey to America. His parents were educated professionals — his father an economist, his mother a teacher. When Vlad was five years old, his family moved to Washington, D.C., where his father took a position at the World Bank. Growing up as the child of Bulgarian immigrants in the United States, Tenev would later describe watching his parents work hard to navigate a financial system that felt inaccessible and opaque — a memory that would shape everything he built.
2005–2011 — Stanford University
Two Mathematicians Meet
Vlad Tenev studied mathematics at Stanford, where he met Baiju Bhatt, another first-generation immigrant and the son of Indian parents who had moved to the United States. Both were struck by a shared observation: Wall Street was a closed club. Professional traders paid minimal or zero commissions to execute trades. Ordinary Americans paid $5–$10 per trade. The wealth-building machinery of the American economy was available to everyone in theory — but only accessible in practice to those who already had money. That paradox would become the founding idea of Robinhood. Tenev also attended UCLA's graduate mathematics program before leaving to co-found his first company.
2012–2013 — Palo Alto, California
The Name, the Idea, the $3.4M Seed
Tenev and Bhatt founded Robinhood Markets in April 2013 — intentionally named for the folk hero who took from the rich and gave to the poor. The product concept was radical for its time: zero-commission stock trading via a mobile-first app. While Charles Schwab, TD Ameritrade, and E-Trade charged $5–$10 per trade, Robinhood would charge nothing. They would monetize through payment for order flow and margin interest — not retail commissions. The idea was met with deep skepticism by established players who insisted free trading was impossible. Sequoia Capital, Index Ventures, and NEA saw the vision differently, providing a $3.4M seed round.
2014–2015 — Launch & Waitlist
1 Million People on a Waitlist
Before Robinhood launched publicly, Tenev and Bhatt put up a simple landing page. Within days, over one million people had joined the waitlist — one of the most viral pre-launch moments in fintech history. The pent-up demand was obvious: ordinary Americans desperately wanted access to the markets without paying a toll for every transaction. When the app launched in March 2015, it was immediately clear this was not a niche product. Robinhood had tapped something deep in the American psyche — the belief that the stock market should be for everyone, not just the wealthy. Competitors initially dismissed the model. Within three years, every major brokerage had been forced to go to zero commissions to compete.
2019–2020 — $11.2B Valuation
The Democratization Accelerates
Robinhood's user base exploded during the COVID-19 pandemic of 2020. With millions of Americans stuck at home, receiving stimulus checks, and looking for ways to participate in the sudden market rally, Robinhood became the on-ramp of choice for a new generation of investors. The company raised money at an $11.2 billion valuation in 2020. For the first time in American history, retail investors were not bystanders — they were participants, and Robinhood was the vehicle. Vlad Tenev's childhood observation — that the financial system served the already-wealthy — was finally being corrected, at scale, by the company he had built.
January 2021 — GameStop Crisis
The Darkest Moment
In late January 2021, a Reddit-fueled short squeeze in GameStop (GME) sent the stock from $20 to $480 in days, squeezing hedge funds and drawing millions of new retail investors into the market. At the peak of the frenzy, Robinhood restricted purchases of GameStop, AMC, and several other stocks due to a collateral call from DTCC — the clearing house — that Robinhood could not immediately meet. The decision ignited a firestorm. Users felt betrayed. Politicians from both parties called for investigations. Tenev was hauled before Congress. The company's IPO plans were complicated. For a brief but agonizing period, Robinhood's founding mission — to democratize finance — seemed to have been violated by its own infrastructure limitations. It was the hardest test of Tenev's career.
July 2021 — NASDAQ IPO
Going Public at $38
Robinhood went public on NASDAQ on July 29, 2021, raising $2.1 billion at a $32 billion valuation — one of the largest fintech IPOs in history. In a remarkable decision that reflected Tenev and Bhatt's values, the company reserved 35% of IPO shares for retail investors through its own platform — unprecedented in IPO history. Wall Street's gatekeepers had always kept IPO allocations for their institutional clients. Robinhood gave its own users first access. The stock initially stumbled after IPO but briefly spiked above $85 in August before settling. The long rebuild — and the true company-building era — was just beginning.
2022 — The Reckoning
Revenue Collapses 25%, Stock Falls 90%
As retail trading volumes collapsed post-pandemic, Robinhood's revenue fell from $1.81B (2021) to $1.36B (2022). The company laid off approximately 30% of its workforce over two rounds. The stock, once above $85, fell to a low of $6.56 — a 92% decline from the all-time high. Critics declared Robinhood a failed company, a meme-stock casualty, proof that commission-free trading was never a real business. Tenev, the immigrant's son who had spent a decade building something he believed in, refused to give up. He restructured the cost base, preserved the engineering talent that mattered most, and began building the product foundation for what would become the super app.
2023–2024 — The Rebuild
Revenue Doubles, Profitability Returns
Tenev's bet on the next cycle paid off. Revenue recovered to $1.86B in 2023 and then surged +58% to $2.95B in 2024 — the company's first full year of GAAP profitability. The product expansion was accelerating: Robinhood launched retirement accounts with a 1% IRA match (no other brokerage offered this), Gold Card in March 2024, prediction markets, 24-hour trading, index options, futures, and international brokerage in the UK. Bitstamp was acquired in June 2024 for $200M — what would prove to be a steal, giving Robinhood 100+ country reach and institutional crypto infrastructure. TradePMR was acquired for access to $40B+ in RIA assets. The Pluto AI acquisition in July 2025 signaled the AI-powered advisory future.
2025 — The Super App Year
Revenue +52%, HOOD Triples, 11 Businesses at $100M+ ARR
In 2025, Robinhood delivered its most consequential year: revenue grew 52% to $4.47B, adjusted EBITDA margin reached 56.4%, and the stock tripled from ~$25 to $153 before pulling back. Banking launched in November 2025 and immediately attracted $100M then $300M then $400M in deposits within weeks. Strategies hit $1B in AUM in 7 months. The Gold Card grew 5x to 600K holders. Prediction markets hit $12B+ in event contracts in their first full year. WonderFi was agreed for Canada. Two Indonesian acquisitions were announced. Bitstamp became MAS-licensed in Singapore. Robinhood Ventures Fund I (RVI) was prepared for a NYSE listing. Vlad Tenev, the Bulgarian-American immigrant's son, had built the most important new financial institution in America.
2026 — The Opportunity
Down 55% — But the Thesis Has Never Been Stronger
As of April 2026, HOOD trades at $68.90 — 55% below its October 2025 high. Crypto weakness and macro de-risking have punished the stock. But the businesses keep compounding: Banking hit $1.5B in deposits by March 2026. Platinum Card launched. RVI listed on NYSE. Family investing launched. The $1.5B buyback was authorized. The GENIUS Act is law. The Clarity Act is advancing. Indonesia and Canada are nearly complete. Cortex AI is rolling out. Robinhood Social is in final testing. Vlad Tenev, at 38 years old, continues to run the company with the urgency of someone who has been underestimated his entire career — and has proven those doubters wrong at every turn.
The Decade Ahead
Building for the $100 Trillion Wealth Transfer
Tenev speaks often about the $100 trillion generational wealth transfer already underway as Baby Boomers pass their assets to Millennials and Gen Z. His thesis: that generation will not use the same institutions their parents used. They will use the app where they already invest, trade, bank, and plan. Robinhood's goal is to be that app for as many people as possible — in America, in the UK, in Europe, in Canada, in Indonesia, and eventually across the world. The child of immigrants who wanted to make the American dream accessible to everyone is building the institution that will carry that dream into the 21st century.
